# Putting It Together: Finance Metrics in Real Decisions

How to use finance metrics together for product decisions.

## Core Truth: Metrics Work in Systems

**Bad:** "Our CAC is $500, which seems reasonable."  
**Good:** "CAC $500, LTV $2,000, payback 8 months, NRR 115%. 4:1 LTV:CAC, fast payback, strong expansion — scale this channel."

## Red Flag Combinations

### Death Spiral
- Churn increasing (3% → 5% → 7%)
- CAC increasing (saturating channels)
- LTV decreasing
- Magic Number declining (<0.5)

**Action:** STOP scaling acquisition. Fix retention. Run cohort analysis.

### Unprofitable Growth
- Revenue growing 50%+
- Gross margin declining (75% → 55%)
- LTV:CAC declining (4:1 → 1:1)
- Burn accelerating

**Action:** Segment by margin and LTV:CAC. Cut low-quality revenue. Raise prices before scaling.

### Concentration Crisis
- Top customer >30% of revenue
- Top 10 >60%
- Roadmap dominated by top customer

**Action:** Diversify immediately. Resist custom requests. Build for broader market.

### Leaky Bucket
- Quick Ratio <2
- NRR <100%
- Expansion <5% of new revenue
- Cohort retention declining

**Action:** Pause acquisition scale. Fix retention and expansion first.

### False Efficiency
- Magic Number >1.5 (looks hyper-efficient)
- CAC increasing QoQ
- Market share flat or declining

**Action:** You're underinvesting. Competitors scaling faster. Invest in channels with good LTV:CAC.

## Three Loops

1. **Acquisition:** S&M → Leads → Customers → New MRR. Metrics: CAC, Magic Number, LTV:CAC.
2. **Retention & Expansion:** Product → Activation → Value → Expansion. Metrics: Churn, NRR, Quick Ratio.
3. **Profitability:** Revenue → Gross Profit → Contribution → Net Income. Metrics: Margin, Rule of 40.

**Healthy:** Loop 1 efficient, Loop 2 retains/expands, Loop 3 converts.  
**Broken:** Inefficient acquisition OR leaking retention OR can't support growth.

## Common Scenarios

**Free tier?** Model: unit economics of free users, conversion rate, payback on converted, CAC reduction.

**Move upmarket?** Model: Enterprise CAC, ACV, gross margin, payback. Will it distract from working segments?

**Churn increasing?** Analyze: cohort churn, segment churn, reasons, time to churn, NRR. All cohorts? Product degraded. Recent only? Acquisition quality or ICP drift.

**Growing revenue but burning more?** Analyze: growth rate, margin trend, LTV:CAC by channel, payback, OpEx growth.

## Meta-Lesson

**Bad:** "We need $10M ARR" → discount heavily, acquire bad customers, destroy unit economics.  
**Good:** "We need sustainable $10M ARR" → LTV:CAC >3:1, NRR >110%, payback <12mo.

Every metric can be gamed. Optimize the *right combination* that represents true business health.

**Good looks like:** Revenue >30% YoY, NRR >110%, LTV:CAC >3:1, Payback <12mo, Gross margin >75%, Quick Ratio >4, Churn <3%, Rule of 40 >40, Top customer <10%, Magic Number >0.75.
